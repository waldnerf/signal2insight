## Key takeaway

Oversight degrades the overseer.

## Framework choice

scr. The paper's value is a reframe that contradicts what the audience currently believes (a person approving an agent's actions is sufficient safety). Top-down would state the fix on slide two with nothing yet earned; SCR spends the complication breaking that belief before the fix is offered, which is what a reframe needs.

## Storyline

1. [cover] AI agents are gaining autonomy, and a human is still supposed to catch what goes wrong
2. [situation] Human in the loop is the standard mechanism for keeping agent output reliable as autonomy grows
3. [complication] Hugging Face researchers reduce the core problem to four words: oversight degrades the overseer
4. [complication] When a reviewer approves out of habit, that approval becomes the signal the agent learns from next
5. [resolution] Enabling appropriate oversight of AI agents requires user-centric solutions drawn from cognitive science, psychology, and user design
6. [cta] Audit one of your agent approval workflows before you assume the human in the loop is still doing the job

## Supporting elements

- [paper] "oversight degrades the overseer"
- [paper] Critical skills degrade, so judgment fades exactly where oversight needs it most.
- [paper] Ineffective oversight creates a feedback loop, and the cycle closes on itself.
- [external, US Joint Commission, 2023] 85 to 99 percent of hospital monitor alarms are false or minor, and staff learn to tune them out.
- [external, Laban et al FlipFlop experiment, 2023] Ten leading models revised a correct answer 46 percent of the time when simply asked to reconsider.
- [external, Bucinca et al CSCW, 2021] Designs that make a person record a judgment before seeing the AI's answer lowered overreliance versus an explanation-only baseline.
