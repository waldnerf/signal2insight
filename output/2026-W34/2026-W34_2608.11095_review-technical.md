PRIMARY TEST
pipeline sketch (from memory):
  1. Instrument the corpus at instruction level (not file level) across 1,867 GitHub repositories, matching instructions across commit history with an exact/normalized/fuzzy cascade -- needed because a line diff can't tell a deletion from a reword, so file-size studies can't isolate what happened to any one instruction.
  2. Characterize the ratchet: instructions grow steadily until a wholesale-rewrite commit clears roughly half the file, then growth resumes faster than before -- needed to show the pattern is bulldoze-then-regrow, not simple accumulation.
  3. Fit the deletion hazard curve and rule out rival explanations -- staleness predicts hazard rising with age (it falls instead); a gamma frailty model shows content fragility explains under a third of the decline; the multi-author interaction shows hazard falls faster on files more people have touched -- needed to pin the cause on lost rationale specifically, since the fix only follows from the correct cause.
  4. Formalize why deletion becomes irrational once the reason is gone: two instructions can jointly cover one hidden requirement, so verifying either is safe to cut requires testing combinations, exponential in instruction count -- needed to explain the mechanism rather than assert it.
  5. Propose the fix: a comment attached at write time naming the failure that prompted the instruction, stripped before the executor sees it -- needed because it collapses the exponential audit to one question.
  6. Build a testbed with a known ground truth by inverting IFEval, hiding the true instruction list as the minimum cover -- needed because excess size is otherwise uncomputable without a known minimum.
  7. Run maintenance with vs. without comments over 15 and 51 rounds under censored, vague feedback -- needed to show the comment arm settles near/below the minimum while the uncommented arm balloons, at matched satisfaction except on the subset of worlds the protocol emptied.
  8. Seed real WildIFEval prompts with 16 distractor instructions and re-run the protocol -- needed to confirm the size reduction translates into recovered instruction-following on real prompts, reproduced under a second judge.
  9. Swap in stronger maintainer models (Haiku 4.5 vs. Opus 5) -- needed to show the ratchet, and the fix's payoff, scale with agent capability (one seed).
  10. Recommend keeping a human in the deletion path -- needed because writing a comment is provably safe, but the pruning protocol overprunes on a real share of worlds, so acting on a comment is where the risk sits.

SLIDE 1 · fixed, no defect
  "In a 51-round run where the smallest workable rule set was known, those notes cut the surplus above it by 99.3%. Separately, on real prompts padded with 16 needless rules, they lifted instruction-following 23.1%." The 99.3% figure is verifiably the T=51 row of Table 2 (excess size +211.3% to +1.4%; the paper's own Introduction attributes 99.3% to "over 51 steps" specifically). The 23.1% figure is verifiably Section 4.3's WildIFEval run seeded with D=16 distractors. "Separately" now marks the clause boundary correctly, and each clause carries its own condition, so a reader cannot come away believing one experiment produced both numbers. This resolves the pass-3 wrong finding cleanly.

SLIDE 4 · minor · dropped log-hazard slope entirely
  "Staleness would mean older instructions get deleted more often. Across 28,426 deletions, deletion instead becomes less likely as an instruction ages." The direction claim is accurate: Figure 5's Nelson-Aalen hazard falls with age (slope -0.032/commit, 95% CI [-0.047,-0.019] excluding zero), and 28,426 is the correct population for that curve. Removing the slope number rather than fixing its unit label is not wrong, since the direction alone is sufficient to falsify staleness. But the slope with its CI excluding zero was the statistical backbone of "this decline is real, not noise," and its removal converts a measurement into a bare directional claim. Not a fidelity problem, but a completeness one worth the writer's attention given they flagged it themselves.

SLIDE 7 · fixed, no defect
  "On the runs where comments kept instructions in place, both groups satisfied constraints equally well." This is now a precise paraphrase of Appendix C.5: the paper splits worlds by whether the protocol ended empty, then reports satisfaction parity holds where the protocol kept instructions and fails where it emptied them. Table 12(b)'s worlds-kept-instructions split shows no-comments at 0.406, comments at 0.399, paired difference -0.007 [-0.031,+0.016] (CI crosses zero), statistical parity, matching "satisfied constraints equally well." The clause is now correctly conditional rather than a blanket claim.
