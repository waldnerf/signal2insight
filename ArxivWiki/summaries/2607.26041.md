# Desktop-Delta Bench: Do Computer-Use Models Understand Desktop GUI Transitions?

arXiv: [2607.26041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26041v1) &middot; Published 2026-07-28 &middot; Recommendation: **Archive** (score 14/25)

_Pre-extraction: this paper has only been screened on its abstract, not read in full yet._

## High-Level Summary

Tests whether computer-use agents can tell what their own last action actually did to the screen. Across eight model families the best exact-match rate on reconstructing an action's effect was about 65%, and identifying which kind of action occurred proved harder than locating it -- click F1 0.96 against 0.76 for drag.

## Key Highlights

- Isolates a capability end-to-end task success hides: rejecting a stale screenshot, confirming progress, and recovering after a failed step.
- The screen an agent sees may be delayed, occluded, transient or unrelated because inference, input, rendering and capture are all asynchronous -- and a misread frame gets carried forward into every later planning step.
- 2,013 human-verified instances from multi-app Linux trajectories across roughly 15 applications and 50 task domains, with cross-trajectory decoy frames as a control.
- Giving the model task context improved decoy detection by 6.9 points but cost 2.2 points on ordinary cases, and error analysis found models systematically copying the order in which frames were presented rather than reasoning about them.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

Desktop automation business cases are built on unattended completion rates, and this identifies why those rates disappoint: the agent cannot reliably verify its own effect, so it cannot self-correct and every error compounds down the trajectory. That reframes the buying question from 'how often does it finish the task' to 'can it tell when it has gone wrong', which is what determines whether you need a human watching. The per-action-type breakdown is directly usable: click-driven workflows are a materially safer starting scope than drag-driven ones.

## Business Applications

- Scope initial computer-use deployments to click-driven workflows and keep drag-and-drop interactions with a human, based on the measured per-action gap.
- Require a state-verification step in agent designs rather than assuming an action succeeded, and budget for the checkpoint cost in the ROI model.
- Use step-level diagnostics like these to choose between vendors, since end-task success rates will not separate models on recovery behaviour.
