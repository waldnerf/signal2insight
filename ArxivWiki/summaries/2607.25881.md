# AI's Capability in Assisting Scientific Research in Physics, Astrophysics, and Cosmology II: Project Planning and Proposal Evaluation

arXiv: [2607.25881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25881v1) &middot; Published 2026-07-28 &middot; Recommendation: **Archive** (score 21/25)

_Pre-extraction: this paper has only been screened on its abstract, not read in full yet._

## High-Level Summary

Eight research projects were each turned into a one-page plan by human researchers and by three LLMs. All 32 plans were then blind-reviewed by four humans and two frontier models. Human reviewers rated AI-written and human-written plans about equally. Both AI reviewers scored the AI-written plans a full point higher on a five-point scale — and identified which was which every single time.

## Key Highlights

- AI reviewers preferred AI-written submissions by roughly one point out of five. Human reviewers showed no such preference.
- Both AI reviewers classified all 32 proposals correctly as human or AI authored. Human reviewers managed 72% and 79%.
- AI-written plans were good enough that humans rated them on par with expert-written ones, so the quality argument for using AI to draft is separate from the bias problem in using AI to judge.
- Small study — 32 proposals, four human and two AI reviewers — and the authors are explicit that the implication is caution rather than a settled effect size.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

Any process where a model shortlists written submissions now has a measurable bias toward submissions that were themselves AI-drafted: bids and tenders, grant and funding applications, internal project proposals, CV screening, RFP responses. The bias is invisible to the people running the process because the model's stated reasons will look reasonable. It also cuts the other way — as candidates and vendors adopt AI drafting, an AI screen will increasingly reward that adoption rather than the underlying merit. The fix is procedural, not technical: keep a human scorer in any evaluation that decides who wins something.

## Business Applications

- Do not let a model score proposals, tenders or applications unsupervised; keep human scoring on any decision that awards work, funding, or a role.
- If AI assists screening, test for the authorship preference directly by scoring known human-written and AI-written samples before trusting the pipeline.
- Reconsider policies that ban AI-assisted submissions while using AI to evaluate them — that combination now has a documented direction of bias.
