# Phantom Gains: Auditing Self-Improvement Against a Measured Null

arXiv: [2608.20290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20290v1) &middot; Published 2026-08-20 &middot; Recommendation: **Priority review** (score 22/25)

_Pre-extraction: this paper has only been screened on its abstract, not read in full yet._

## High-Level Summary

Whether an AI model has genuinely 'self-improved' is increasingly judged by which individual problems it gains or loses, not average accuracy -- and that measurement is fragile. Auditing three rounds of self-training against a frozen control run through an identical pipeline, this paper finds seven distinct measurement failures, each of which flips a reported finding once the control is properly accounted for.

## Key Highlights

- Several of the seven failures are standard practice today, meaning many published or vendor-claimed self-improvement results may be measurement artifacts rather than real capability gains.
- One failure alone (a single greedy decode) manufactures apparent capability changes on a model that was never even trained.
- The methodology (compare against a frozen control pushed through the identical pipeline) is a directly reusable checklist for auditing any self-improvement claim.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

Any organization evaluating a vendor's or an internal team's claim that an AI system 'improved itself' now has a rigorous, reusable checklist for telling a real gain from a measurement artifact -- directly relevant to AI procurement and internal capability claims alike.

## Business Applications

- Apply the audit methodology to internal fine-tuning or RL pipelines before reporting capability gains upward.
