# Stemma: Induced Decision Regions Reveal LLM Provenance

arXiv: [2607.25880](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25880v1) &middot; Published 2026-07-28 &middot; Recommendation: **Archive** (score 11/25)

_Pre-extraction: this paper has only been screened on its abstract, not read in full yet._

## High-Level Summary

A black-box method for testing whether a suspect model descends from yours. Instead of comparing response style — which shifts under fine-tuning and deployment settings — it maps open-ended outputs into a fixed decision space and measures whether the source model's decision regions were inherited. It reaches 0.967 AUC across 770 source-suspect pairs and 0.995 AUC across 91 deployment configurations.

## Key Highlights

- 0.967 AUC and 87.8% true positive rate at a 1% false positive rate over 770 pairs drawn from 56 public checkpoints.
- Holds up across diverse weight transformations, which is what defeats surface-level fingerprinting approaches.
- 0.995 AUC across 1,260 pairs covering 91 deployment instances, so it survives inference-time serving differences.
- Requires only black-box access — no weights, no cooperation from whoever is serving the suspect model.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

Model lineage is becoming a contractual and legal question rather than a research one. Three concrete situations need an answer: whether a competitor fine-tuned weights you own, whether a licensee breached its terms, and whether the API you are paying premium rates for is actually serving the model named in the contract. Until now the honest answer was that you could suspect but not demonstrate it. The audience here includes IP counsel and procurement, not just the security team.

## Business Applications

- Verify that a model API is serving the contracted model before renewing, rather than trusting the vendor's description.
- Establish a fingerprint baseline for proprietary models before releasing or licensing them, so lineage can be tested later.
- Give IP and licensing teams a technical basis for enforcement discussions about derived models.
