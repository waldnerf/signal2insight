# InsufficiencyBench: Evaluating LLM legal advice on underspecified user queries

arXiv: [2608.20220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20220v1) &middot; Published 2026-08-20 &middot; Recommendation: **Review fully** (score 18/25)

_Pre-extraction: this paper has only been screened on its abstract, not read in full yet._

## High-Level Summary

Legal AI benchmarks assume users ask fully-specified questions, but real users routinely omit facts that materially change the legal answer. InsufficiencyBench is the first benchmark testing whether LLMs recognize when a legal query lacks the information needed to answer it responsibly, using 202 attorney-annotated items across 24 US jurisdictions.

## Key Highlights

- No frontier model tested exceeds F2 = 0.46 at recognizing missing legally-material information -- most models guess rather than flag the gap.
- Built and annotated by practising attorneys across six legal domains and 24 jurisdictions, not synthetic or crowdsourced.
- Defines a reusable taxonomy of eight missing-element categories across three structural failure modes, giving legal-AI evaluators a concrete checklist.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

Any product offering LLM-based legal guidance carries real liability exposure if the model confidently answers an underspecified question instead of asking for the missing facts -- this is the first rigorous test of exactly that failure mode.

## Business Applications

- Run InsufficiencyBench (or its taxonomy) against a legal-AI vendor before procurement to check for silent over-confidence.
- Add a 'flag missing information' requirement to internal LLM legal-assistant evaluation criteria.
- Use the eight-category taxonomy as a design checklist when building any LLM tool that answers underspecified professional questions (legal, medical, financial).
