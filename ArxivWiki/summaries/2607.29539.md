# ARB: A Matched Authorship-Rewriting Benchmark Dataset for AI-Text Detector Evaluation

arXiv: [2607.29539](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29539v1) &middot; Published 2026-07-31 &middot; Recommendation: **Review fully** (score 20/25)

_Pre-extraction: this paper has only been screened on its abstract, not read in full yet._

## Key Highlights

- The two strongest detectors caught 91.2% and 93.5% of text a model wrote from scratch.
- The same detectors caught only 30.8% and 15.1% of human-authored text that a model had rewritten -- a drop of 60 to 78 percentage points.
- When a model rewrote its own output, detection held up far better (78.3% and 83.0%), so rewriting alone does not explain the collapse -- human authorship underneath does.
- Two of the five detectors tested stayed below 3% detection in every condition.
- Tested at a 1% false-positive point, so the numbers reflect what you would get if you cared about not falsely accusing people.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

Many organisations have written AI-use policies that assume detection is possible, and some have bought tooling on that basis. This says the tooling works well on the case that rarely occurs and fails on the case that dominates real use. Any control, policy or disciplinary process resting on detector output is resting on something that misses roughly seven out of ten instances of the most common usage pattern -- and the number that gets quoted in vendor material is the one measured on the wrong scenario.

## Business Applications

- Re-examine any AI-use policy whose enforcement mechanism is a detector, since the enforcement step does not work for assisted writing.
- Require vendors to report detection rates on human-authored-then-rewritten text specifically, at a stated false-positive rate, before purchase or renewal.
- Shift from detection-based controls to disclosure-based or provenance-based ones for work product where authorship genuinely matters.
- Avoid using detector output as evidence in any process with consequences for an individual.

## Headline

AI-text detectors catch over 90% of text a model wrote from scratch, and under 31% of your own writing after a model tidied it up.

## Why triage flagged this

A control many organisations already rely on, measured against the way people actually work. Detectors scoring 91.2% and 93.5% on directly generated text fall to 30.8% and 15.1% when the same models rewrite human-authored source -- a 60 to 78 point collapse -- while holding up under LLM-on-LLM rewriting (only 10 to 13 points lost). That asymmetry is the finding: the standard benchmark measures the case that almost never happens. Method is sound for the claim: 1,800 matched source texts, four open-weight generators, four variants per item, evaluated at a strict 1% false-positive point rather than a flattering threshold. States in one sentence without ML vocabulary and directly invalidates a policy assumption. Held at Review fully rather than Priority: detection tooling is a narrower enterprise concern than the pipeline's core design-and-deploy mission.
