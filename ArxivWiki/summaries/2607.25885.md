# A Machine-Learning-Based Gas Lift Optimization Workflow for Unconventional Fields

arXiv: [2607.25885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25885v1) &middot; Published 2026-07-28 &middot; Recommendation: **Archive** (score 15/25)

## High-Level Summary

An automated workflow for setting gas injection rates across wells: a machine-learning model forecasts the performance curve, and Bayesian optimisation finds the best injection rates within facility capacity limits. It runs on production history the operator already has, needing no downhole gauges or multi-rate well tests. A 30-well pilot delivered over 5% average production uplift, and it is now deployed across more than 200 wells.

## Key Highlights

- Over 5% average production uplift in a 30-well pilot across five well pads, then full deployment on 200+ wells.
- Requires no new instrumentation — it uses historical production time series instead of downhole gauges or multi-rate tests, which is what made it economic.
- Capacity constraints are handled inside the optimisation rather than as an afterthought, so the recommended rates are actually executable.
- The methods themselves are conventional; the contribution is the deployment design, not the algorithm.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

Most industrial AI business cases die on data acquisition: the model needs sensors that cost more than the projected benefit. This one was built around that constraint instead of arguing for it, which is why it reached 200+ wells rather than staying a pilot. That is the transferable decision — before funding an instrumentation programme, ask whether the existing operational history is sufficient. It also stands as a rare example of a reported production number rather than an offline benchmark improvement.

## Business Applications

- Test whether existing operational history can support an optimisation model before approving a sensor or instrumentation investment.
- Structure throughput optimisation as forecast plus constrained search, so recommendations respect facility limits and can be acted on directly.
- Use the pilot-to-fleet path here as a template for scoping industrial AI: prove uplift on a small asset group, then scale on the same data foundation.
