# Daedalus-150M: A Convolution-Attention Hybrid Designed for CPU Inference

arXiv: [2608.20210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20210v1) &middot; Published 2026-08-20 &middot; Recommendation: **Review fully** (score 18/25)

_Pre-extraction: this paper has only been screened on its abstract, not read in full yet._

## High-Level Summary

Most small language models are just scaled-down large-model architectures retrofitted for cheap hardware afterward. Daedalus-150M instead designs the architecture around a fixed target from the start (one user, one token at a time, 4-bit weights, an ordinary CPU) -- and the resulting 150M-parameter model beats several models trained on 3-6x more data.

## Key Highlights

- Uses full attention in only 6 of 18 blocks; the other 12 use short convolutions with constant memory regardless of conversation length.
- Beats GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, OPT-125M, GPT-Neo-125M (all trained on far more data) and exceeds MobileLLM-125M's published score despite far less training data.
- Trained from scratch on 59.9B tokens with the architecture chosen specifically for CPU deployment, not adapted after the fact.

## Why It Matters for Enterprise AI

For any enterprise weighing GPU-dependent hosted inference against cheaper on-prem or edge deployment, this is direct evidence that a properly CPU-native small model can be competitive without a hardware upgrade or a data-scale disadvantage.

## Business Applications

- Evaluate CPU-native small models like this as a lower-cost alternative to GPU-hosted inference for narrow, well-scoped tasks.
- Use as a benchmark when negotiating hosted-inference vendor pricing (a competitive open small model exists).
- Consider for edge/on-device deployments where GPU access isn't available.
